§2

Cleaning — six stages, two new filters

Kept short on purpose; two of the six are new, the rest are correctly-done standard practice.

  1. Extraction + script-invariant language ID — robust to code-mixed sentences that a whitespace-based LID model misreads as garbage or the wrong language.
  2. Transliteration-canonicalized dedupNEW — near-duplicate detection normally runs per script, so the same article scraped once in Devanagari and once as romanized Hinglish looks like two different documents. We transliterate every document to a shared phonemic key before MinHash, so cross-script duplicates get caught too.
  3. Per-language quality filtering + Unicode NFC normalization.
  4. PII removal — DPDP Act 2023 patterns (Aadhaar/PAN/UPI) + Indic NER.
  5. Nirapeksh (निरपेक्ष, "impartial") consistency checkNEW — every identity-bearing sentence (caste-coded surname, religion-coded name, regional marker) gets a counterfactual swap; documents where a paired model's output shifts for reasons unrelated to the substitution feed a training-time consistency loss, not just an eval-time probe.
  6. Benchmark decontamination (12-gram/fuzzy overlap against every benchmark in §4) + a memorization audit gate before any release.
§3

Tokenizer — ~180K vocab, and why that number

Anchored to two real 2026 papers, not a round guess.

BrahmicTokenizer-131K (arXiv, May 2026) proved a 131K retrofit of o200k_base can match English fertility (1.235) while cutting Indic tokens 26.7% — but it explicitly dropped nine non-Brahmic scripts, so Urdu, Kashmiri, Sindhi and Santali are out of scope for it. IndicSuperTokenizer (OpenReview, Oct 2025) proved a second, independent lever: "superword" multi-word merges beat raw vocab growth, gaining 39.5% over LLaMA-4 through procedure, not size. We start from both, then add the two pieces neither did.

Sandhi-BPE (सन्धि — the grammatical term for sound-merging at word boundaries, apt since BPE is itself pairwise merging): one shared tokenizer, not one per language. Devanagari sits inside the Brahmic-core budget alongside the other eight Brahmic blocks, exactly as in BrahmicTokenizer-131K — Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, Sanskrit and Konkani all draw on the same allocation. What we add is specific to the four Dravidian languages, where BrahmicTokenizer's own numbers show the smallest gain (Tamil +15.79%, against Odia's +76.79%): an existing morphological analyzer biases merge selection toward real morpheme boundaries instead of raw frequency, with a confidence-gated fallback to plain BPE so the floor is never worse than known-good behavior. One merge table, applied uniformly — a requirement, not a preference, since Hinglish and Tanglish interleave scripts inside a single sentence.

§5

Five ideas we think are actually new

This is where the real word-budget goes — everything above this line is done correctly; this is what's different. Filter the 19 named ideas below by where they sit in the model lifecycle.

DPI-Gym

NEWAgentic

A simulated-environment suite (à la WebArena/OSWorld) built around India's actual Digital Public Infrastructure — UPI, DigiLocker, ONDC (its protocol specs are genuinely open on GitHub, low legal risk to simulate), CoWIN, e-Shram, Aadhaar-eKYC, FASTag, and RBI's Account Aggregator framework — that doubles as an SFT/RL trajectory generator and a held-out benchmark.

Realism knobs harder than WebArena's clean web: injected rate-limiting, OTP timeouts, payment idempotency, vernacular utterances that must map to correct API parameters, and simulated 2G/low-bandwidth conditions forcing retry/USSD-fallback behavior. Rewards are execution-verified against the simulator's resulting ledger state, never "looks plausible" — and it's simulated-only, never live rails or real user data. The method — simulate a nation's DPI stack and its real operating constraints — generalizes directly to Singapore's SingPass, Brazil's Pix, Estonia's X-Road: a genuine benchmark contribution, not a one-off toy.

Sandhi-BPE

NEWTokenizer

Morphology-seeded superword tokenizer for the four Dravidian languages — full mechanics in §3. The contribution is procedural, not architectural: a concrete, testable extension of two real 2025/26 papers (BrahmicTokenizer-131K, IndicSuperTokenizer) rather than a reinvention, targeting exactly the case both left hardest.

Rupantar Chains

NEWरूपांतर — "transformation"Data

Synthetic data generation that goes past every reference submission's plain back-translation-with-COMET/chrF gating. An English fact or reasoning chain is translated, then a culturally-grounded rewriter re-expresses it in Indian idiom, units and analogy — transcreation, not translation — then NLI-verified for faithfulness back to the source. The result is natively Indian in framing, not just in language, which mere translation-quality gating can't produce.

Drishtikon-Bench

NEWEval

The memo's namesake contribution. Instead of accuracy-style worldview probes, it measures a perspective displacement score: KL-divergence between the model's answer distribution on ambiguous, ethnocentric-prone questions (discovery attribution, historical framing, "typical" X) versus a Western-default baseline model — gated by a factuality floor so displacement toward an Indian frame never overshoots into inaccuracy or majoritarianism. Turns "India-first" from a vibe into a number.

Samvidhan-CAI

NEWसंविधान — "constitution"Alignment

A Constitutional-AI-style critique/revision RLAIF loop where critique prompts are drawn from specific, named Articles of the Indian Constitution (14, 15, 19, 25, 29, 30 — equality, expression, religious freedom, minority/cultural rights) and the Directive Principles, each with legal-linguist-validated native-language templates — more rigorous and reproducible than a generic "constitutional framing" gesture.
